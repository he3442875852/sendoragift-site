Look for clarity before promises

A strong corporate gift supplier should ask questions before promising a fixed answer. Product choice, quantity, logo method, packaging, destination and deadline all affect the project. If a supplier gives a universal price, MOQ or lead time without reviewing those details, the buyer may face changes later. Clear communication is often more valuable than a fast but vague promise.

Evaluate the process, not only the product photo

Product photos can inspire ideas, but corporate gift success depends on execution. Buyers should ask how logo placement is reviewed, whether packaging layout is checked, what sample options are available and how shipment details are confirmed. A supplier that can explain the approval path is usually easier to work with than one that only lists product names.

Photos on the website should be understood as representative gift set concepts, reference solutions or customization directions unless a page clearly verifies a completed order. This protects buyers from assuming a product image proves a specific customer case. The better supplier conversation focuses on how a similar direction can be adapted to the buyer's audience and constraints.

Questions procurement teams should ask

Before choosing a supplier, procurement teams can ask what information is needed for a quote, how MOQ is determined, which logo methods fit the chosen materials, what packaging options are realistic, how samples are approved and what shipping information is needed. The answers should be specific enough to guide next steps without inventing unsupported guarantees.

A good supplier selection process also checks response quality. Do they notice missing details? Do they explain tradeoffs between product selection, packaging and delivery? Do they make it easy for internal teams to approve the next step? These signals matter because corporate gifts often involve several stakeholders and a deadline.

Decision notes for supplier due diligence

Supplier due diligence should separate verified company information from project-specific claims. A buyer can review the legal company name, contact details and external profile, then still ask for product, logo, sample and shipping confirmation for the actual project. Both layers matter.

A helpful supplier should also be willing to explain uncertainty. If MOQ, price, lead time or shipping cannot be confirmed before product details are selected, the answer should say so clearly. This is a strength, because it prevents procurement from building a plan around unsupported assumptions.

For distributor or sourcing-agent projects, supplier selection should also consider how clearly the partner can support resale communication, buyer approvals and project notes without exposing unsupported claims.

FAQ

Answers for Buyers

What should I check before choosing a corporate gift supplier?

Check company identity, communication clarity, product fit, logo review process, sample options, packaging support, shipping planning and whether claims are supported.

Should I choose the supplier with the lowest first quote?

Not automatically. A low quote may omit packaging, logo details, sample needs or shipping assumptions. Compare what is included and what still needs confirmation.

Why should a supplier ask many questions?

Custom corporate gifts depend on product selection, quantity, branding, packaging and delivery. Questions help avoid wrong assumptions before quote or sample approval.

Does Sendora Gift publish fixed MOQ or price tables?

No. MOQ and price depend on the selected products, quantity, logo method, packaging and destination. Sendora Gift confirms those details after reviewing the buyer requirements.

Can buyers request samples before bulk production?

Yes. Sample options can be discussed after the product combination, artwork and packaging direction are clear. Sample timing and cost are confirmed project by project.

Can the product combination be changed?

Yes. Buyers can start from a reference gift set and adjust items, logo placement, packaging or delivery requirements based on the audience and budget.
