How early should you start a corporate gift order?

Start from the date the gifts must be received and work backward through shipping, packing and inspection, production, sample approval and artwork. Confirm each stage for the selected products, quantity and destination. Production time alone does not include buyer approvals, sample delivery or final transit.

Think in stages instead of fixed lead-time claims

Public lead-time tables can mislead buyers because corporate gift orders vary widely. A ready-made product with a simple logo is different from a multi-item gift set with custom packaging, insert cards and split international delivery. A useful timeline describes the decisions that must happen in order: inquiry, product direction, quote review, artwork preparation, mockup, sample decision, bulk approval, packing review and shipment planning.

Sendora Gift confirms timing after the products, logo method, packaging, quantity and destination are known. This protects buyers from relying on a generic promise that may not match their project. The earlier the team shares its event date or employee launch date, the easier it is to identify which steps need simplification.

Order stages and the approval needed to move forward

Keep one written schedule with a named approval owner. The stage lengths are confirmed for your order; this sequence is a checklist, not a fixed delivery promise.

How to keep the timeline realistic

Buyers can keep a project moving by preparing logo artwork early, naming one approval owner and deciding which details are flexible. If the deadline is tight, it may be better to choose available product colors, simpler packaging or fewer decoration methods. If presentation is more important than speed, the team may allow more time for sample review and packaging refinement.

A timeline is a shared planning tool, not just a supplier promise. Procurement, HR, marketing and logistics all affect it. Clear internal decision dates help Sendora Gift recommend options that match the real project constraints.

Decision notes for deadline planning

A timeline becomes more reliable when the buyer separates hard deadlines from preferred milestones. An event date, launch date or employee onboarding date may be fixed. Product style, package structure, logo method or delivery route may be flexible. Knowing which details can move helps protect the parts that cannot.

Internal approval is often the hidden timeline risk. Procurement, brand and HR may each need to review a different part of the project. Buyers can reduce delay by agreeing who approves the product, who approves artwork, who approves packaging and who confirms shipment details before the inquiry is sent.

A written timeline also helps buyers decide when to stop changing the product list. Once artwork, packaging and destination details are approved, late edits should be treated as schedule risks.

Questions before you order

Answers for Buyers

Does production lead time include shipping?

Do not assume it does. Ask for production, inspection and packing, dispatch and delivery estimates separately. Also confirm when the production clock starts, because artwork changes, sample revisions and incomplete order details can delay that starting point.

Can you confirm an arrival date before the products are chosen?

A reliable arrival plan needs the product mix, quantity, branding, packaging, destination and approval process. Share your required in-hand date first, then ask which combination and route can be reviewed against it before committing to a schedule.

What can I simplify if an event date is close?

Ask about available product colors, fewer items, one logo version and standard packaging. These changes may reduce work, but availability and timing still need confirmation. Faster shipping only helps once the products and packing are ready.

Should sample delivery be included in the schedule?

Yes. If a physical sample is required, allow for making it, shipping it, internal review and any revision. Confirm the sample recipient and approval owner early so the delivery does not arrive without someone ready to review it.

What happens if artwork changes after approval?

The change needs a new review of setup, sample approval, packaging and production timing. Identify the exact revision and ask whether the previous schedule still applies. Keep one written approved version to avoid different teams using different files.

Which date should I provide for an event order?

Provide the date the gifts must be received and ready for use, plus the event date if different. Include venue or warehouse receiving hours, advance-delivery restrictions and the receiving contact so the plan covers arrival and handover.
