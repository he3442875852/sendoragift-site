Which logo method fits your product?

Choose the logo method by the product surface, artwork and desired finish. Printing can provide visible brand color; laser engraving suits selected metal surfaces; debossing creates a recessed mark on suitable PU; embroidery adds a stitched mark to suitable textiles. Confirm the exact material and sample result before production.

Choose the method around the item, not just the logo

A logo that looks excellent on a notebook may not behave the same way on stainless steel, fabric, silicone, paper, ceramic or coated plastic. Corporate gift branding should begin with the product material and the user experience. Some items need a subtle mark, while trade show giveaways may need higher visibility. A premium client gift may benefit from a restrained engraving or debossed packaging rather than a large printed logo.

Sendora Gift can discuss common options such as screen printing, UV printing, laser engraving, embroidery, embossing, debossing, labels, stickers, sleeves, insert cards and branded gift boxes. The available method depends on the selected product and supplier capability for that item. The final recommendation should be made after reviewing artwork, quantity and sample requirements.

For a metal pen, compare engraved contrast with a printed logo. For a PU folder, compare a subtle recessed mark with a more visible foil or print. For a textile bag, review the artwork at its actual decoration size.

Before sampling, send vector artwork where available, brand color references, the requested logo dimensions and its position on each item. Keep small text and fine lines open for a sample-based review. Compare mockups and physical samples.

Approval details that prevent branding mistakes

Buyers should provide vector artwork when possible, plus brand color references and any clear-space rules. Logo size, position and color should be checked on a mockup before sample production. If the logo includes thin lines or small text, the decoration method may need adjustment. A sample or detailed production photo can help confirm whether the mark looks acceptable on the actual item.

Logo methods affect MOQ, sample cost, production process and sometimes packaging. They should not be treated as a last-minute add-on. The earlier the method is discussed, the easier it is to choose products that support the brand style and approval process.

Decision notes for logo approval

Logo approval should consider how the recipient will use the product. A large event logo may work on a tote bag but feel too loud on an executive notebook. A subtle engraving may feel premium but may not be visible enough for a trade show giveaway. The right decision depends on audience, channel and brand tone.

Buyers should also confirm whether the product or the packaging should carry the main brand message. A smaller item logo combined with a stronger box, sleeve or insert card can preserve usefulness while still making the campaign feel custom. This choice should be reviewed before sample approval.

Questions before you order

Answers for Buyers

Is laser engraving better than printing for a metal pen?

It depends on the finish you need. Engraving can create an understated mark on a suitable metal or coating, while printing can provide a more visible brand color. Review the actual pen surface, contrast and logo detail before selecting either method.

What is the difference between embossing and debossing?

Embossing creates a raised impression; debossing creates a recessed impression. The selected material and construction determine what is possible. Ask to see the effect on the chosen PU item or packaging material, especially where the logo has thin lines or small text.

Can I use the same logo method on every item in a gift set?

Only if each surface supports it. A metal pen, PU notebook and textile bag may need different methods to achieve a coordinated look. Approve the logo size, position and finish separately for each item, with one shared artwork reference.

Can the logo color exactly match my screen?

A screen image is not a physical color approval. Supply a brand color reference and review the selected ink, foil or thread on the actual material. Surface color and texture can affect the result; engraving follows the material response rather than a printed color.

Which artwork files should I provide?

Provide vector artwork such as AI, EPS, SVG or a vector PDF where available, with fonts outlined or supplied appropriately. Include color references and the desired size and position. If you only have a raster image, send the highest-resolution original for a suitability review.

Can I brand only the gift box or card?

Yes, packaging branding can be reviewed while the products remain unbranded. Confirm the box, sleeve, label or card artwork and how it looks with the selected products. Packaging has its own minimum quantity and approval steps.
