Corporate Gift Purchase Order Checklist for Procurement Teams

Turn an approved custom gift quotation into a clear purchase order by linking every product, logo, packaging and delivery instruction to one controlled specification.

Prepare the RFQ FirstRequest a Project Review
Quick answer:

A corporate gift purchase order should identify the exact products and quantities, agreed commercial terms, approved artwork revisions, branding instructions, packaging components, sample references, delivery details, inspection expectations and change-approval contacts. Use the PO as the commercial control document and attach a specification pack for details that do not fit clearly on one line.

A purchase order sits between an accepted quotation and production. Its job is to remove ambiguity, not to repeat every sales conversation. This is especially important for a gift set because one order can include several products, different logo methods, a custom box, an insert card and multiple packing instructions.

If the project is still at the sourcing stage, begin with the corporate gift quote checklist. The steps below apply after the buyer and supplier have agreed on a product direction and are preparing the production record.

1. Match the Purchase Order to the Final Quotation

Reference the supplier quotation number and revision date. Confirm that the PO uses the same currency, quantities, item descriptions, included customization, packaging scope and shipping basis as the accepted quote. If a line differs, resolve it before release rather than relying on a message sent later.

  • Buyer PO number and supplier quotation reference
  • Buyer and supplier legal or billing details used for the order
  • Currency, line quantities, agreed unit prices and total
  • Payment and shipping terms exactly as agreed
  • Named commercial contacts on both sides

2. Give Every Product an Exact Reference

A line such as “custom gift set” is too broad for production control. List each product or refer to an attached bill of materials. Record the model or approved reference, material, dimensions, stock color, required accessories and individual packing where those details affect the order.

For substitutions, state that any changed product, color, material or accessory requires written review. A reference image can help communication, but it should not be the only specification.

3. Control the Artwork and Branding Version

Link each decorated item to the approved artwork filename, revision, logo dimensions, placement, color reference and branding method. A gift set may use engraving on a pen, printing on a tote and debossing on a notebook, so one general instruction is not enough.

Artwork reference lineProduct reference · artwork filename and revision · logo size · placement · color reference · branding method · approval date

Keep the production artwork and approved visual mockup together. If a logo or product surface changes, issue a revised approval rather than overwriting the old file.

4. Specify Packaging as Separate Components

List the gift box, insert, sleeve, sticker, card, bag and any product-level packing as separate controlled components when they are part of the order. Record dimensions or approved references, artwork versions, item orientation and packing quantities.

  • Gift box or mailer type and approved size reference
  • Interior insert layout and product orientation
  • Insert-card copy, language and artwork revision
  • Individual protection, sleeves or bags
  • Master-carton quantity and label instructions

5. Link the PO to Sample Approval

A PO records the order, while an approved mockup or sample records the expected result. Reference the approved sample, artwork proof or packaging layout by date and version. If only part of the set was sampled, state which elements remain subject to review.

The separate corporate gift sample approval process explains how to review products, logo execution and packaging before bulk production.

6. Record Delivery and Packing Requirements

Use the destination and shipping terms that were actually agreed. Include the required delivery point, recipient or warehouse contact, postal code, requested date, carton marks and any packing split. If final freight details are still pending, label them as pending instead of inserting an assumption.

For several offices, events or buyer warehouses, attach a controlled distribution list showing quantities by destination. Do not place personal recipient data in a widely shared document; use an access-controlled file and the minimum information needed for fulfillment.

7. Define Inspection and Change Control

State what must be checked before release: product reference, quantity, appearance, logo position, packaging, carton condition or other agreed criteria. Avoid vague wording such as “best quality” when a measurable specification or approved reference is available.

Changes after approval should be recorded in writing. Identify the affected line, specification revision, commercial impact, schedule impact and approver. Production should continue from one clearly approved version.

A clear PO does not guarantee that every future change is avoidable, but it creates a shared baseline for review. Procurement can compare the final order against one controlled record instead of reconstructing decisions from separate chats and images.

Corporate Gift Purchase Order FAQ

What should a corporate gift purchase order include?

Include the agreed product references, quantities, commercial terms, approved artwork and branding instructions, packaging specifications, sample approval references, delivery details, inspection expectations and the contacts authorized to approve changes.

Should logo files be attached to the purchase order?

Reference the approved artwork filename and revision in the purchase order or an attached specification pack. Keep the production artwork and visual mockup together so the supplier and buyer review the same version.

Can a purchase order replace sample approval?

No. A purchase order records what is being purchased, while sample or artwork approval confirms the expected product, logo and packaging result. Both records should use matching references.

How should changes after purchase order approval be handled?

Record the requested change in writing, identify the affected specification, confirm any commercial or schedule impact, and issue an approved revision before production continues.
